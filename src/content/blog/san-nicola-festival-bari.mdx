Once a year, Bari goes out to bring its saint home from the sea. Thousands of people on boats, rowing boats, anything that floats, crowd around a golden statue and escort it back to shore. It's been happening like this for centuries, and it's the moment you understand that San Nicola, in Bari, is not just any saint: he's the city itself.

The **Feast of San Nicola** is Bari's most important festival. To an outsider it can look like folklore; to the Barese it's memory, identity and an appointment no one misses. Here's what happens, and why.

## When and what is celebrated

The feast is held every year on **7, 8 and 9 May** and commemorates the **translation of the relics** of Saint Nicholas: in **1087**, according to tradition, a group of Bari sailors (the city's memory speaks of sixty-two men) brought the saint's bones to Bari from **Myra**, in Lycia (modern Turkey). Everything springs from that arrival: the basilica built to house them, and the festival that re-enacts it every spring.

That's why San Nicola "arrives from the sea": because it was from the sea that he really arrived.

---

## The historical procession (7 May)

The feast opens with the **historical procession**: hundreds of costumed figures in Byzantine and medieval dress — tunics, turbans, velvets, the saint's golden icon — parade through the streets of the old town, re-enacting the arrival of the relics. It's not just any costume parade: it's Bari staging, for itself, the day that made it famous in the Christian world.

---

## San Nicola arrives from the sea (8 May)

This is the festival's signature image. The **statue of San Nicola** is loaded onto a boat and taken out to sea, followed by a flotilla of fishing boats and anyone who can put out to water. The saint spends the day on the sea and returns in the evening, welcomed by an enormous crowd. It's a **sea procession** that symbolically recreates that ancient landing — and one of the most intense collective rituals in all of Puglia.

---

## The procession through the streets

Alongside the rite on the sea, the **statue is carried shoulder-high in procession** through the streets of the old town, between walls of people, old women waiting at the front, hands raised as it passes. Old photos and today's are almost identical: the clothes change, not the gestures. It's the proof that here devotion isn't spectacle, but continuity.

---

## The Basilica di San Nicola

Everything turns around the **Basilica di San Nicola**, in the heart of old Bari: one of the great Romanesque churches of southern Italy, built at the end of the 11th century precisely to house the newly arrived relics. The façade, tall and severe as a fortress, is one of the city's symbols. It was a building site begun to give the saint a worthy home: the basilica was born with and for his bones.

---

## The crypt and the Holy Manna

Below the basilica is the oldest and most charged part: the **crypt**, a forest of columns over a mosaic floor, where the saint's relics rest. It's one of the few places in the world where **Catholics and Orthodox pray to the same saint**, in the same space: pilgrims come to Bari from Greece, Russia and all the Christian East.

Here, too, one of the most unusual rites takes place: every year, on the morning of **9 May**, a clear liquid is collected from the relics, the **Holy Manna** (Santa Manna), held precious by the faithful. It's an ancient rite that has been repeated, unbroken, for centuries.

---

## San Nicola, that is, Santa Claus

Here's the detail that throws outsiders: the San Nicola of Bari is the same **Saint Nicholas** who, transformation after transformation, became the **Santa Claus** known all over the world. The 4th-century bishop, protector of children and sailors, is the root of the Christmas figure. So when you stroll through old Bari, you're walking past the "real" Santa Claus.

---

## How to experience it and practical tips

- **The dates**: 7-8-9 May. The historical procession on the 7th, the sea procession on the 8th, the Holy Manna on the 9th.
- **Arrive early**: the city is packed, especially along the seafront and in old Bari.
- **Respect the rites**: the basilica and crypt are places of living worship, not just monuments.
- **Outside the feast too**: the basilica and crypt can be visited all year, the quietest way to understand where it all comes from.

## Frequently asked questions about the Feast of San Nicola

### When is the Feast of San Nicola in Bari?

The main feast is held every year on 7, 8 and 9 May: the historical procession on the 7th, the sea procession on the 8th, the drawing of the Holy Manna on the 9th. San Nicola is also celebrated on 6 December.

### What is the sea procession of San Nicola?

On 8 May the statue of San Nicola is taken out to sea on a boat, followed by a flotilla, and brought back to shore in the evening. It symbolically re-enacts the arrival of the relics from the sea in 1087.

### What is the Holy Manna of San Nicola?

It's a clear liquid that every year, on 9 May, is collected from the saint's relics in the crypt of the basilica. It's considered miraculous by the faithful and is the object of a very ancient rite.

### Why is San Nicola linked to Santa Claus?

Saint Nicholas of Myra, a 4th-century bishop and protector of children, is the origin of the figure of Santa Claus, which evolved over the centuries through northern European traditions. His relics have been in Bari since 1087.

### Can you visit the crypt of San Nicola all year round?

Yes. The basilica and the crypt are open for worship and visits all year. The crypt, where the relics rest, is also a place of prayer for Orthodox faithful.
