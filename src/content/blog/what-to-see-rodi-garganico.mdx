Look at Rodi Garganico from below and you see two Puglias at once. Below, the beach with its umbrellas and the seaside holiday everyone pictures. Above, a centuries-old white town, perched on the promontory, with narrow lanes and houses facing the Adriatic.

Most visitors stop at the first of the two. But the best of Rodi is in the climb: into the old town, and into a story made not only of sand, but of citrus — oranges and lemons that made the Gargano famous long before beach tourism.

## What to see in Rodi Garganico

### 1. The white old town on the promontory

The heart of Rodi is the **old town**, clinging to a rock spur sheer above the sea, on the northern Gargano coast. White houses, climbing lanes, views that open suddenly onto the blue: it's the classic Mediterranean architecture of the Gargano seafaring towns, where everything is built to sit high and watch the sea.

Getting lost in the lanes, climbing to the viewpoints and looking out over the Adriatic is the best way to understand why a town grew up there.

---

### 2. The Gargano citrus

Here's the part almost no tourist knows: **Rodi Garganico is citrus country**. This stretch of coast is the home of the **Gargano orange** and the **Femminello Gargano lemon**, both protected by the IGP mark — intensely fragrant fruit, grown in groves that reach almost down to the sea.

It's not just any food detail: for centuries the Gargano citrus was a prized export, and Rodi was one of the ports it left from. When you walk among the orange groves you sense, quite literally, what the historic economy of this place was made of.

---

### 3. The beaches

At the foot of the town stretch Rodi's **sandy beaches**, comfortable and serviced, among the busiest of the northern Gargano. East and west, the coast offers further stretches of sand and quieter spots. It's one of the most practical beach resorts in the area: sea and old town just steps apart.

---

### 4. The ferry to the Tremiti Islands

Rodi Garganico is one of the main **departure ports for the Tremiti Islands**: in high season, ferries and hydrofoils leave from here for the archipelago. It's a great base for anyone who wants to dedicate a day to the Tremiti, with their crystal-clear sea and caves, and be back by evening.

---

### 5. How to find your way around and when to go

The geography is simple: **the white town high on the promontory**, **the beaches below**, **the harbour** you take to the Tremiti.

- **For the sea**: high summer, but reckon with the August crowds.
- **For the town and the citrus**: lovely all year, including spring, when the groves are in bloom.
- **For the Tremiti**: leave early and check the timetables, which change with the season.

Rodi pairs well with a tour of the northern Gargano: [Peschici](/en/blog/what-to-see-peschici/), [Lesina](/en/blog/what-to-see-lesina/) and the coastal villages.

---

### 6. What to eat in Rodi

Citrus, first of all: a **Gargano lemon granita** is almost compulsory, and the citrus sweets and liqueurs tell the story of the land. Alongside, the Gargano's seafood — fresh fish, shellfish — and the produce of the hinterland. Climb up into the town with a granita in hand: that's the right way to visit it.

## Frequently asked questions about Rodi Garganico

### What is Rodi Garganico famous for?

Rodi is known for its white town above the sea, its beaches and the Gargano citrus — the orange and the Femminello lemon, both IGP, grown in groves near the coast.

### Can you go to the Tremiti from Rodi Garganico?

Yes. Rodi is one of the main embarkation ports for the Tremiti Islands: in high season, ferries and hydrofoils run. It's a day trip: leave early and check the timetables.

### Are the beaches of Rodi Garganico nice?

Yes, they're comfortable, serviced sandy beaches, among the busiest of the northern Gargano, with the advantage of the old town just steps away.

### When is the best time to visit Rodi?

For the sea, high summer (with the August crowds to reckon with). For the town and the groves, spring and early autumn too, which are quieter.

### What is the Gargano citrus?

It's the Gargano orange and the Femminello Gargano lemon, two IGP-protected varieties with an intense fragrance, historically grown along this coast and once an important export.
